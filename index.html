    About
    DEEP FIELD
    An open-source universe explorer: the Solar System to the edge of the observable universe, on real data, in the browser.

    Deep Field is a fork of Your Eyes In Space by Berk Comba / BigBrains, hosted by Joachim Leiros. It is not affiliated with or endorsed by BigBrains. This fork leaves out the original's guided tours, Academy lessons, events and narration (not covered by the code licence), and its streamer and multiplayer modes (which run on the original's servers). For those, visit youreyesinspace.com.

    Data sources and licences

    Star catalogue: HYG v4.1, AT-HYG v4.0 (David Nash, Astronexus) — CC BY-SA 4.0; Compiled from the Hipparcos, Yale BSC, Gliese and Tycho-2 catalogues. Nearby stars: Gaia Catalogue of Nearby Stars (GCNS, Gaia EDR3). This work has made use of data from the European Space Agency (ESA) mission Gaia, processed by the Gaia Data Processing and Analysis Consortium (DPAC). Exoplanets: NASA Exoplanet Archive (PSCompPars), operated by the California Institute of Technology under contract with NASA under the Exoplanet Exploration Program. Exoplanet surfaces are depictions based on the measured radius, mass and temperature. Planetary orbital elements: NASA/JPL (Standish, Keplerian Elements). Spacecraft positions: JPL Horizons (approximate, October 2026). Spacecraft 3D models: NASA 3D Resources (public domain). Mission planner: patched-conic approximation (Lambert, Hohmann, single gravity assist); science-fiction ships are fan-made, nominative representations of the respective works; the models are original and schematic. Surface imagery: Earth — NASA GIBS (Blue Marble Next Generation, VIIRS Black Marble); Moon — NASA Moon Trek (LRO WAC mosaic); Mars — NASA Mars Trek (Viking MDIM 2.1); Mercury, Venus, the moons of Jupiter and Saturn, Ceres and Vesta — NASA Solar System Treks (MESSENGER, Magellan, Galileo, Cassini and Dawn mosaics; the Venus surface is radar imagery). All are NASA public domain and streamed directly from NASA servers. Galaxy catalogue: NED / literature values. Black holes: literature values (GRAVITY, EHT, Gaia DR3 etc.); rendering is real-time ray tracing in the Schwarzschild metric. Nebula, cluster and remnant landmarks are shown at their real positions as three-dimensional clouds derived from NASA/ESA Hubble (public domain) and ESO (CC BY 4.0) photographs; those without a photograph are procedural representations. Pulsars: ATNF Pulsar Catalogue values; beam geometry is schematic. Procedural stars, galaxies and the cosmic web are generated deterministically and must not be confused with real catalogues. Scientific catalogues: asteroids and comets — NASA/JPL Small-Body Database; pulsars — ATNF Pulsar Catalogue (Manchester et al. 2005); open clusters — MWSC (Kharchenko et al. 2013); globular clusters — Harris (2010 edition); planetary nebulae — Strasbourg–ESO Catalogue (Acker et al. 1992); supernova remnants — Green (2019); H II regions — WISE Catalog of Galactic H II Regions (Anderson et al. 2014); nearby galaxies — Updated Nearby Galaxy Catalog (Karachentsev et al. 2013); galaxy survey — 2MASS Redshift Survey (Huchra et al. 2012); brown dwarfs — UltracoolSheet (Best et al. 2024); multiple stars — Multiple Star Catalogue (Tokovinin 2018) and the USNO Sixth Catalog of Orbits of Visual Binary Stars (ORB6). Catalogues accessed through NASA HEASARC and CDS VizieR.

    IDeep Field

    The universe, as it is.

    From the Solar System to the edge of the observable universe, everything from real data, at true scale, as it is right now. In your browser, free.

    Saturn, behind this text, is exactly where it is at this minute.

    IIData

    Nothing here is invented.

    The planets stand where the NASA/JPL ephemerides place them. The stars come from Hipparcos and Gaia, the galaxies from NED and SIMBAD. Wind the clock forward and the sky turns the way the real one will.
